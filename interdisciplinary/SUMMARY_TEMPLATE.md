# Summary Template for Interdisciplinary Papers

## Naming Convention

Summary files are named: `YYYY-MM-DD-author-et-al-year-summary.md`

Example: `2026-06-02-fedorenko-et-al-2024-summary.md`

## Workflow

1. Download PDF to `interdisciplinary/`
2. Convert: `markitdown paper.pdf -o paper.md`
3. Write summary following the structure below. Use LaTeX for math if needed: `$...$` for inline, `$$...$$` for display equations
4. **Iterate**: Each paper read is also an opportunity to refine this template.

## Note on Interdisciplinary Papers

Interdisciplinary papers vary widely in structure: some are empirical, some are theoretical, many are review/perspective articles that synthesize evidence across fields. Unlike economics papers which typically follow a "model → experiment → results" pipeline, interdisciplinary papers often build arguments from **evidence blocks** — collections of findings from different disciplines or methodologies that converge on a claim.

The template below is designed for **argument-driven papers** (perspectives, reviews, opinion pieces). For interdisciplinary papers that contain formal models or original experiments, borrow the relevant block templates from the economics template.

## Summary Structure

### Overview

- **Paper type**: What kind of paper (perspective, review, empirical, theoretical, etc.)
- **Central claim/thesis**: The main argument in one sentence
- **Number of evidence blocks**: How many distinct bodies of evidence support the argument
- **Evidence block purposes**: What each block does
- **Block relationships**: How blocks build on each other to support the thesis
- **Key debate**: What opposing view(s) the paper argues against
- **Conclusion**: Final takeaway

### High-Level Summary

In 3-5 sentences: what is the core insight, why does it matter, and what makes this paper's argument compelling (or not)?

### Evidence Blocks (in paper order)

#### Evidence Block Template

- **Claim**: What this block argues
- **Discipline/Method**: Where the evidence comes from (neuroscience, linguistics, evolutionary biology, behavioral experiments, computational analysis, etc.)
- **Key evidence**: The most important findings, studies, or data points (be specific: cite key studies, sample sizes, methods where available)
- **Logic**: How this evidence supports the claim
- **Limitations/Caveats**: What the authors acknowledge or what you notice as weaknesses

### Counter-arguments & Responses

- What are the main objections to the thesis?
- How does the paper address them?

### Open Questions

- What does the paper explicitly flag as unresolved?
- What questions do you have after reading?
